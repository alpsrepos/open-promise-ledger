# The Open Promise Ledger: Method

Version 0.2, draft for comment. Changed from version 0.1 (human first) on 26 November 2026: an AI system now makes the first reading and the editor checks every value; the change log lists every changed section. Author and editor: Ismet Alp Ceylan, a student at Bocconi University. This is not a publication of the university. The editor alone is responsible for this text and for every record.

## 1. Research question and scope

Sustainability-linked bonds (SLBs) are bonds whose payments change, usually through a higher coupon, if a sustainability target stated in their terms is not reached (ICMA 2024). The Ledger records each measurable target an issuer set in an instrument's own documents, called a "promise" in this text, and what the issuer or its named external reviewer later published about it. It is a record of data that issuers have published about their own targets, and of statements by issuers and their external reviewers. It does not rate, score or rank issuers or instruments, does not assess their sustainability profile, does not decide whether an instrument is green or sustainability-linked, does not propose or suggest any investment decision or strategy, and gives no opinion on the value or price of any instrument.

Research question for phase 1: for SLBs listed on Euronext markets with a target observation date in 2024 or 2025, what did issuers promise, what did they later report, was the reported figure externally reviewed, and did the issuer state that the payment change in the terms took effect? Results describe in-scope issuer groups in this frame only, and, because only near-term targets are in scope, say nothing about targets due later.

## 2. Sources and frame

Only documents published by the issuer or its named external reviewer are sources: terms, final terms, prospectuses, frameworks, annual and sustainability reports, SLB progress reports and the reviewer's reports on them, and regulated announcements. News, data vendors, ratings, third-party summaries and AI output are not. Identifiers come from the GLEIF register and the registered Euronext file. Each source is recorded with URL, hash and page (Appendix E.2).

The frame is every bond typed "Sustainability-Linked Bond" in the Euronext ESG bonds list downloaded on a stated date, completed from one public web-archive capture of the list where it can be read, because bonds redeemed or delisted before the download may be missing. Frame membership is decided by these lists alone.

## 3. Sample

The sampling unit is the issuer group: all issuing entities in the frame that share an ultimate parent in the GLEIF relationship records. A group is in scope if at least one of its bonds was listed by 31 December 2023, the entity whose reports the terms name has its legal address in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, and its terms contain a target with an observation date in 2024 or 2025.

Groups are put in random order with registered software and seed (Appendix D) and screened in that order, without screening the frame in advance. The first three in-scope groups are a declared pilot, used to develop the procedure and kept out of all agreement and validation figures. Wave 1 continues until ten in-scope groups reached by the order have been coded, pilot included (about seven in the random sample); stratum-only groups do not count toward the ten. The non-pilot groups form a simple random sample without replacement drawn by sequential screening. Every bond reached receives a disposition, and the number of groups screened is reported. In-scope groups based in a pre-registered list of Central and Eastern European countries form a stratum, coded up to four in wave 1 in registered order and never pooled with the random sample.

## 4. Units and variables

One target can sit in many bonds, so the record has four linked units: the **instrument** (one bond with all its ISINs); the **promise**; the **link** (one per instrument and promise, holding the consequence in that bond's terms and the status); and the **observation** (one reported figure or statement per promise and reporting period, with its publication date and the external review in the reviewer's own words).

For an SLB, a promise is each sustainability performance target defined in the terms or final terms, for each observation date named there; targets found only in frameworks, second-party opinions or marketing are not recorded. Two bonds share a promise only if issuer group, metric, perimeter, direction, promised value, baseline and observation date are identical. "Promise" names a target stated in an instrument's documents; it does not mean the target is a legal obligation, or that not reaching it is a breach or a default. The effect of not reaching a target is only what the terms state.

Changed targets and restated figures are added, never overwritten (Appendix E.4). Field definitions and codes are in the data dictionary.

## 5. Status labels

Each link carries one label, always shown with its basis; a statement by the issuer or its external reviewer takes precedence over the editor's comparison of figures. Labels are not weighted, scored, coloured or combined, the rules are tested in this order, and the numbers do not rank them.

> **Figure 1. Order of the status rules.** Ended before observation date? → Target lacks a number or date? → Observation date in the future? → Statement found? → Comparable figure? → Figure not comparable? → Deadline open? → Search incomplete? → No report found by the editor.

1. **Ended before observation date**: the bond was redeemed, matured or exchanged, or stated to be in default, before the observation date.
2. **Cannot be compared (promise)**: the target has no number or no date.
3. **Not yet due**: the observation date is still to come.
4. **Stated as reached** / **Stated as not reached**: the issuer or its external reviewer has stated publicly whether the target was met or whether the payment change takes effect. "On track" is not a statement.
5. **Reported figure reaches** / **does not reach the promised value**: no statement was found after a logged search, and a figure exists with the same unit, perimeter, period and calculation method; it is compared in the stated direction using the terms' own rounding rules.
6. **Cannot be compared (figure)**: the only figure differs in unit, perimeter, period or method.
7. **Awaiting report**: nothing found, and the reporting deadline in the terms (otherwise 270 days) is still open.
8. **No figure recorded yet**: the deadline has passed and the search for absence is not complete. Not named.
9. **No report found by the editor**: all four search steps found nothing. This states nothing about whether a report exists, whether one was required, or the issuer's conduct.

Rule 1 applies per link; rules 2 to 9 per promise. Rules for restatements, conflicting statements and changed targets are in Appendix E.5.

## 6. Coding procedure and use of AI

> **Figure 2. How a record is made.** Document pack → two AI readings in new chats → quote check → comparison of the two readings → editor checks every value at the page and decides each status → AI blind coding and AI audit → issuer notice → release.

**AI first, editor checks.** An AI system makes the first reading of every instrument. The editor reads the target and payment-change sections of the terms himself, checks every core value against the quoted page (through the AI's translation where he does not read the language, flagged), corrects it where needed, and decides each status and its basis by the rules of section 5. No value enters the record without the editor's check at the page. He also reads the target and results sections in full to find anything both readings left out.

**AI readings.** The AI is the first reader, not an independent coder, and its agreement with the editor is not a measure of reliability, because the editor checks its values. An AI system (Claude, by Anthropic; the model identifier is given with each run) reads the documents twice, in two new chats given this Method, the data dictionary, a registered prompt and the documents, in an account whose memory, chat search and data-use settings are recorded and disclosed with each release, and must give a page and exact quote for every value or answer "not found in the supplied documents". A script checks that each quote is on its page; a value whose quote is not found is never entered. A script, never the AI, compares the two readings; the editor checks every value at the page whether or not they agree, and logs each change and its reason. Use of AI follows current reporting guidance (Flemyng et al. 2025; Törnberg 2024).

**Other uses of AI.** The AI also fills descriptive fields, drafts translations, and may support screening a group out only by quoting a clause the editor reads; the editor checks each, and these fields are kept out of agreement figures. Files reserved against text and data mining are never sent to it; the editor reads such instruments himself; no AI checks their lines, and they are flagged "checked by the editor only". Issuer replies and non-public information are never sent to it either (Appendix E.6). The editor decides every record and holds editorial responsibility; the AI is not an author.

**Establishing absence.** "No report found by the editor" needs four logged steps, including a request to the issuer and an independent search by a second person; an AI search never counts as a search, so while no second person takes part such lines keep "No figure recorded yet" (Appendix E.6).

## 7. Technical validation

**AI blind coding.** Release 2027.1 has no outside human coder or auditor (editor's decision of 27 November 2026). An AI blind coder, a model other than the one that makes the readings, codes all instruments of at least three non-pilot groups from the documents alone, without seeing the record or the readings, under the conditions in Appendix E.7. Its agreement with the final record and with the unchecked first reading is reported as in E.7, labelled "agreement between an AI blind coder and the final record", and is not a human validation.

**Validation of the AI reading.** The prompt is tuned on the reference cases, the pilot and a trap run in which a results report is withheld, then frozen. Because the blind coder is an AI, the marks (90 per cent, 90 per cent, zero) are not applied, and every release states "AI reading not validated".

**AI audit.** An AI auditor, a model other than the reading model and the blind coder, checks every link's core fields and status against the source, sees editor-AI differences unlabelled, and reports to the editor, who resolves every finding at the page. It does not approve the release; the editor does, and every count carries the label "Provisional. Read first by an AI system and checked by one editor. Not yet checked by a second person."

## 8. Registration, replies, data and code

The frame, seed and stratum rule are deposited with a DOI before the pilot, and the protocol before any non-pilot group is coded; later changes are logged as deviations. Issuers are notified before release and may correct records with a public source; changes from replies are flagged and counted (Appendix C). Records, data dictionary, AI run files and code are archived on Zenodo with a DOI per release, under CC BY 4.0 and MIT; source documents are not republished but can be retrieved and checked by URL and hash.

## 9. Inference, reporting and limitations

Shares by status are given per promise and per link, with issuer-clustered bootstrap intervals, and only once the random sample alone has ten groups; before that, a release gives plain counts. No per-issuer totals or rankings are published, and every count carries the label "Provisional. Read first by an AI system and checked by one editor. Not yet checked by a second person." (Appendix E.9).

Limitations: the frame covers one exchange group's list, and earlier redeemed bonds may be missing; only near-term targets are in scope; the record repeats what issuers published and cannot tell whether a figure is correct, and reporting may depend on the result; issuer replies can change records in one direction; documents the editor does not read unaided are checked through the AI's translation, flagged on each line; the first wave has about ten groups, so all figures are indicative; one editor wrote and applies the rules, and checks values the AI found first, so his check may lean toward the AI's reading; no outside person codes or audits the release, so the blind coding and the audit are made by AI models and do not validate the AI reading; and AI outputs vary between runs, may be influenced by the account's earlier conversations, and models are withdrawn.

## Appendix C. Process rules and fixed texts

**Pointer request** (sent as soon as a line cannot be completed): "I could not find the report of [metric] for [year]. If it is public, please send the link."
**Notice.** Sent from an openpromise.org address to the published investor-relations address and the debt or treasury contact (by post to the registered office if both bounce), with the lines, quotes, page numbers and planned publication date. It states: "Every line below was first read by an AI system from your published documents and then checked by me against the page; where a document is not in a language I read, I checked it through the AI's translation, and those lines are marked. I decided every status. Please do not send information that is not already public. Your reply will be published in full up to 150 words, without personal names or contact details. I accept corrections only with a public source." One reminder on working day 8. No notice is sent, and no reply period runs, from 15 December to 6 January or in August. A longer reply is returned with a request for 150 words or fewer. If nothing arrives, the line shows "no reply received by [date]"; a later reply is handled as a correction request. A reply that appears to contain non-public information is not used or published, and the sender is told.
**Corrections.** A public form opens a ticket linked from the issuer's page; the line is marked "under review" within one working day and left out of statistics until resolved; the fix or reasoned refusal follows within five working days.
**Refresh.** Open lines are refreshed within 60 days of the issuer's next annual report and at least every 12 months.
**Above 60 links.** The AI auditor checks every line where the two AI readings differed or the editor changed a value, every "No report found by the editor" line, every "Stated as not reached" and "Reported figure does not reach the promised value" line, and a random 30 of the rest.
**Outside persons.** Release 2027.1 has none: the blind coder and the auditor are AI models (E.7). If a later release has one outside person, that person codes blind first and checks second if time allows; otherwise they check, blind coding is cut to three instruments, and the release states "agreement between the blind coder and the final record: too few units to report" and "AI reading not validated".
**Wording rule.** The project's own text never uses: verified, certified, assured, safe, risk, avoid, recommended, failed, broke, greenwashing, or forms derived from them, except inside quotations and titles of source documents. (This sentence is exempt from the automated word scan.)
**Method changes.** A change takes effect only after a public proposal, 14 days for comment, and a written outside comment, for example from a named academic reader or the auditor; once a method panel exists, by its published decision.

## Appendix E. Detailed rules

This appendix holds the full rules summarised in sections 2 to 9. Sections 1 and 8 have no detailed rules beyond the main text; any change to a rule is made in both places and listed in the change log. Where the main text and this appendix differ, this appendix applies.

### E.2 Sources and frame

**Admissible sources.** Documents published by the issuer or its named external reviewer: terms and conditions, final terms, prospectuses, frameworks, annual and sustainability reports, SLB progress reports and the external reviewer's reports on them, and regulated announcements. An unaltered copy hosted by a third party is admissible if the issuer refers to the same document elsewhere; both locations are recorded. News, data vendors, ratings, third-party summaries and AI output are not sources. If the terms cannot be obtained, a target may be taken from a regulated announcement or prospectus that names the ISIN, or identifies the bond by issuer, currency, amount, coupon and maturity matching the Euronext file, and quotes the target, flagged "target not read in terms"; otherwise the instrument is recorded as "documents not obtained".

**Identifiers.** Entity names, LEIs, legal addresses and parent relationships come from the GLEIF register; ISINs, listings and the issuer's instrument label from the registered Euronext file. The project records the issuer's own label and does not reclassify instruments.

**Source records.** Each source is held with URL, retrieval date, SHA-256 hash, page (or, for sources without pages, the heading under which the quote appears), a web-archive link where a capture works (for HTML sources the hash is of the evidence copy saved at retrieval, and the record names that copy), and a private evidence copy. Quotes are limited to the sentence or table row that states the target or the figure. Quoted text and source documents are outside the project's open licence; rights in them stay with their holders. Evidence copies are not shared, except with the auditor where the original link no longer works.

**Frame.** Every bond typed "Sustainability-Linked Bond" in the Euronext ESG bonds list, downloaded on 26 November 2026 (frame/REGISTRATION.md); the file hash and the reuse terms are recorded. Because Euronext's terms of use do not allow the list to be republished or compiled into a database without written permission, the list and the frame tables drawn from it are not published unless that permission is given: their SHA-256 hashes, the code and the GLEIF inputs are published, and the frame tables are deposited on Zenodo with restricted access, so that anyone who downloads the same list can rebuild the frame and the order and check the hashes. Instruments that are coded are identified from their own documents. Frame membership is decided by this list alone. The list shows bonds listed on the download date, so bonds that matured, were redeemed or were delisted earlier may be missing. The editor looks for the first public web-archive capture of the list dated on or after 31 December 2023. If it can be read, Sustainability-Linked Bonds found there and absent from the current file are added with the flag "added from archived list", and the number added is reported; the capture's address and date and a saved copy with its hash are registered with the frame. If no capture exists, or it cannot be read within two hours, this is recorded and the limitation stands.

### E.3 Sample

**Sampling unit.** The issuer group: all issuing entities in the frame that share an ultimate parent in the GLEIF relationship records, or the entity itself where none is reported. The legal issuer, any guarantor, and the entity whose reports the terms name for the targets (the reporting entity) are recorded for every bond.

**Inclusion.** A group is in scope if at least one of its bonds in the frame was listed on or before 31 December 2023, its reporting entity has its legal address in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, and its terms contain at least one target with an observation date in 2024 or 2025. Inclusion is checked group by group in the order below; the frame is not screened in advance.

**Order and stopping rule.** Groups are sorted by parent LEI and put in random order with the software, function and seed given in Appendix D; the sorted list, code and seed are deposited with the frame. The editor screens groups in that order. The first three in-scope groups form a declared pilot, used to develop the procedure; their records stay in the Ledger, flagged, are excluded from all agreement and validation figures, and every count is shown with and without them. Wave 1 continues down the order until ten in-scope groups reached by the order have been coded, pilot included. Stratum groups coded only because of the stratum rule do not count toward the ten. The release states the realised numbers: groups screened, pilot groups, random-sample groups (expected about seven) and stratum-only groups. If fewer than three non-pilot groups that are not reference cases are available for the blind coder, wave 1 continues down the order until there are three. Non-pilot groups reached by the order form the random sample: a simple random sample without replacement of in-scope groups, drawn by sequential screening. The number of groups screened is reported. Every bond reached receives a disposition: included; screened, out of scope; documents not obtained; documents in a language the editor does not read (only where no AI reading is allowed); or not reached in the order.

**Central and Eastern European stratum.** In-scope groups whose reporting entity has its legal address in a country on the list in Appendix D form a stratum. If there are more than four, the first four in the registered order are coded in wave 1 (a random subsample of the stratum) and the rest in wave 2. A group reached by both rules is coded once, counted in both and flagged. Stratum results are reported on their own and never pooled with the random sample.

**Prior knowledge.** Before registration the editor studied three reference cases (Appendix A). If a reference group is drawn, it is coded, flagged "reference case", and kept out of the blind coding and the held-out set.

### E.4 Units and variables

One target can sit in many bonds. The record therefore has four linked units, each defined in the data dictionary: the **instrument** (one bond with all its ISINs: issuer, guarantor and reporting entity, dates, size, and state, each state such as "redeemed early" or "in default" only as stated in a public document, with date and source); the **promise**; the **link** (one per instrument and promise); and the **observation** (one reported figure or statement per promise and reporting period).

**Promise.** For an SLB, a promise is each sustainability performance target defined in the terms or final terms, for each observation date named there. Targets that appear only in a framework, a second-party opinion or marketing material are not recorded. Two bonds share one promise only if issuer group, metric, perimeter, direction, promised value, baseline (where relative) and observation date are all identical in their terms. "Promise" is the name this record gives to a target stated in an instrument's documents; it does not mean the target is a legal obligation, or that not reaching it is a breach or a default. The effect of not reaching a target is only what the terms state. Each promise records the exact quote with an unofficial English translation drafted by the AI and checked by the editor, marked "AI translated; the original prevails", its source, metric, unit, direction (at least, at most, equal), perimeter, baseline, promised value, the terms' own rounding or calculation rules, the observation date and the reporting deadline. The observation date is the last day of the period over which the terms measure the target; where the terms name only a year, 31 December of that year; the terms' own label (for example "Reference Year 2025") is recorded separately. Items that may or may not fall under this definition are flagged "boundary case", decided by the editor against this text, and shown to the auditor.

**Link.** Links are made only for instruments in the frame; other instruments carrying the same target are listed on the promise as "also linked outside the frame" and not counted. Each link holds the consequence in that instrument's terms (for example a coupon step-up), the status (section 5), and whether the issuer or paying agent stated that the payment change took effect, coded with the first that applies: stated as applied (a document dated on or after the payment date says it was paid or the new rate applied); announced (an issuer document says it will take effect; the date is recorded); not triggered (target stated or reported as reached); not yet due (target not reached, no issuer statement on the payment change, and the first affected payment date is after the status date); not stated. Caps, "only once" rules and joint triggers across targets are recorded on the instrument and quoted.

**Observation.** Observations are recorded for the period that ends on the promise's observation date, and for each later restatement of that figure; figures for other years are kept in the notes and not coded. Each observation records the reported value and unit, any statement by the issuer or external reviewer on the target, the publication date, taken in this order: the date printed in the document, the regulated-filing timestamp, the earliest web-archive capture (file metadata and URL paths are recorded but do not count); its relation to the deadline in the terms (where the terms tie the deadline to an event and give no date, such as the date a report is required to be published, the fallback of 270 days after the observation date is used and the promise is flagged "deadline relative in terms"), coded "dated on or before the deadline", "first dated evidence after the deadline" or "cannot be established"; "first dated evidence after the deadline" is used only where the date is printed in the document or on a regulated announcement, and a date from a web capture alone gives "cannot be established"; the code records dates only and does not state that a report was late or that any duty was not met; and the external review as the reviewer itself describes it (for example "limited assurance"), with provider and standard, quoted.

**Changes after issue.** A changed target, baseline or calculation method is never overwritten: old and new values are recorded with date, stated reason and manner of change. A change counts as made under the terms only if the issuer's document cites the terms' recalculation clause and the review that clause requires. Restated figures are added as new observations; the earlier figure stays visible.

### E.5 Status labels

Each link carries one status label, shown as text after the promised and reported figures together with its basis. A label is never shown or exported without its basis. Where the issuer or its external reviewer has made a public statement, the label repeats it; only where no statement exists does it record whether the reported figure reaches the promised value, marked as the editor's comparison. Labels are not weighted, scored, coloured or combined into any measure for an issuer or instrument. The rules below are tested in order and the first that applies is used; the numbers do not rank the labels (Figure E1). Every label carries its status date.

1. **Ended before observation date**: the instrument was redeemed, matured or exchanged, or stated in a public document to be in default, before the observation date.
2. **Cannot be compared (promise)**: the target as written has no number or no date.
3. **Not yet due**: the observation date is after the status date.
4. **Stated as reached** or **Stated as not reached**: a public document published by the status date contains a statement by the issuer or its external reviewer that the target was or was not met, or that the payment change will or will not take effect. Wording such as "on track" is not a statement. The label names who made it (basis "issuer statement" or "reviewer statement") and quotes it.
5. **Reported figure reaches the promised value** or **Reported figure does not reach the promised value**: only where the editor's logged search (section 6) found no statement, and a reported figure exists with the same unit and perimeter as the promise, for the period ending on the observation date, measured under the definition in the terms or a change made under the terms. Where the promise is flagged "target not read in terms", the definition condition is met if the reporting document states that the figure is prepared for the terms of the notes concerned, or for the terms of a class of instruments that it describes and that includes the notes (for example the group's sustainability-linked bonds with targets on that metric); the editor quotes that sentence. A detail of method in the reporting document counts as a change of method only if that document or an earlier issuer document says the method changed. The two figures are compared in the stated direction, applying the terms' own rounding and calculation rules and nothing else. Basis: "comparison of figures by the editor; no issuer statement found".
6. **Cannot be compared (figure)**: no statement exists, and the reported figure differs in unit, perimeter, period or calculation method. A missing baseline value matters only where the target is relative and the issuer reports neither the baseline nor the percentage change.
7. **Awaiting report**: nothing has been found and the reporting deadline has not passed. The deadline is the one in the terms; where they give none, 270 days after the observation date (Appendix D).
8. **No figure recorded yet**: the deadline has passed, nothing has been found, and the four steps of section 6 are not complete. Lines with this label are not named.
9. **No report found by the editor**: the deadline has passed and all four steps found no figure and no statement. This means only that the editor did not find a figure or statement in the places listed on that date. It does not state that no report exists, that one was required, or anything about the issuer's conduct.

Rule 1 is applied per link, using that instrument's state; rules 2 to 9 per promise, for all its remaining links. Where several figures exist for the period, the label uses the first the issuer published, and a link whose label would differ on a restated figure is flagged. Where a statement and a comparison of figures would differ, the statement sets the label and the link is flagged "statement and figures differ". Where a statement relies on a target or definition changed without the terms' clause being cited, the statement sets the label, the link is flagged, and the comparison against the original target is recorded as an observation. A target changed under the terms is compared with the target in force on the observation date.

> **Figure E1. Order of the status rules.** Ended before observation date? → Target lacks a number or date? → Observation date in the future? → Issuer or reviewer statement found? → Comparable figure found? → Figure not comparable? → Deadline still open? → Four-step search incomplete? → No report found by the editor.

### E.6 Coding procedure and use of AI

> **Figure E2. How a record is made.** Document pack built → two AI readings in new chats → quote check → comparison table → editor checks every value at the page and decides each status → AI blind coding and AI audit → issuer notice → release.

**AI first, editor checks.** For each instrument the AI reads the documents first and proposes the core fields (quote, metric, direction, promised value, observation date, consequence, reported value, the issuer's or reviewer's statement) with page and quote. The editor checks every core value against the quoted page, corrects it where needed, and decides the status and its basis by the rules of section 5. A value the editor changes is flagged "changed by the editor" with the reason. Because the editor checks values the AI proposes, he also reads in full, for each instrument, the section of the terms that defines the targets and observation dates, and the section of the report for the observation year that gives the result, and records whether the AI's list of promises and observations is complete. A promise or observation found by the editor and by neither AI reading is flagged "added by the editor", and the number of such items is reported. The first reading is the earlier of the two runs by timestamp, fixed before the editor sees either reading. The AI does not propose statuses; the editor decides each status from the checked values. Where a document is in a language the editor does not read unaided, he checks it through the AI's translation; the lines it supports are flagged "read via machine translation", and values and quotes are always taken from the original. Before using rule 5, the editor runs a logged search for a statement in the issuer's investor pages, the annual and sustainability reports for the observation year, regulated announcements and any named external reviewer's report. This search is also step 1 of establishing absence and is logged once. If a place in the search cannot be reached, the log records it, and rule 5 may still be used with the flag "search incomplete: [place]"; that place is searched again at the repeat search before release.

**AI readings.** The AI is the first reader. It is not an independent coder, and its agreement with the editor is not a measure of reliability, nor of its accuracy, because the editor checks its values. The AI system (Claude, by Anthropic; the model identifier is given with each run) reads the documents twice, each time in a new chat inside a dedicated project, given this Method, the data dictionary, the registered prompt and the documents, with web search and connectors not used. The account's memory, chat search and data-use settings are left as they are; they are recorded with dated screenshots at the start of each extraction period and disclosed in each release, and the prompt instructs the AI to use only the supplied documents. The protections against influence from earlier conversations, and against values the AI invents, are the two separate readings, the quote check, the editor's check of every value at the page, and the blind coding, held-out validation and trap run. Files over 100 pages are cut to a pack of pages containing a registered search term, plus two pages either side (terms in Appendix D); comparisons are labelled "within the document pack". The AI answers in the table format of the data dictionary, with document, page and exact quote for every value, or "not found in the supplied documents". A script checks each quote on its cited page after normalising spaces, line breaks, hyphenation and ligatures; a passed check shows that the quote exists, not that the value is right. A value whose quote is not found is marked "unsupported" and is never entered on the AI's word.

**Other uses of AI.** The AI also fills issue, maturity and first call dates, size, currency and the publication date of each observation, and drafts the unofficial translations; the editor checks each against the page, and these fields are marked "AI-filled, human-checked" and kept out of all agreement figures. To screen a group out, the AI may be asked one registered question (does the terms' text contain a target observation date in 2024 or 2025?); a group is recorded as out of scope only after the editor reads the clause the AI quotes, and a "yes" gives the editor nothing else. The AI also drafted the data dictionary, scripts, site pages and notice texts, which the editor reviewed. Before any file is sent to the AI service, the editor records the website's terms of use, any reservation against text and data mining and any click-through. A securities-law disclaimer (for example a click-through for persons outside the United States, or a general sentence in it against copying or forwarding the materials) is recorded with its text but is not a reservation; this is an editorial decision, disclosed with each release, because such disclaimers name neither text and data mining nor AI use. A third-party copy of a document marked against distribution or commercial use is not used where the issuer publishes the same document; the issuer's copy is used. A reservation is found where the terms, or a machine-readable signal (robots.txt, a TDMRep file, or technical blocking of automated access), name text and data mining or automated or AI use, or prohibit downloading or storing the content; a copyright notice alone is not a reservation. Each check is logged with a saved copy of the page and its hash; where one is found, no file of that instrument is sent to the AI, for reading or for translation. The editor extracts its core fields himself from the page, its lines are flagged "no AI reading" and "checked by the editor only", because no AI may check them and Release 2027.1 has no outside person. If the editor does not read the document's language unaided, the instrument is recorded as "documents in a language the editor does not read". Issuer replies before publication and non-public information are never sent to an AI. The data-use setting under which inputs may or may not be used to train models is recorded and disclosed. The editor decides every record, holds editorial responsibility for everything published, and is responsible for every error. The AI is not an author.

**Check and reconciliation.** A script or spreadsheet formula, never the AI, compares the two AI readings. The editor checks every core value at the page, with no AI conversation open, whether or not the two readings agree; he resolves every difference and logs each change and its reason. After the editor's check, a check panel of four separate AI checks runs, each in a new chat, each working only from the saved files, none seeing another's output, and none run by the model that made the two readings: (1) a value check, which checks every value and quote against the cited page; (2) a blind re-reading by a third model, which reads the terms and the results sections without seeing the record, lists every target, date, threshold, margin, condition and result, and is compared with the record by script, so that omissions are found independently; (3) a blind status check, which receives the record with the statuses, bases, consequence codes, statement quotes, status dates and flags removed (the blinding is done by script), finds the statements itself in the saved files, including image pages, applies section 5 in order and is compared with the record by script; (4) an adversarial check, which looks for the most likely error, any breach of the wording rule and any claim in the issuer notice that the record does not support. Before the panel, scripts recompute every rule a script can check (identifier check digits, codes, date order, the sign and direction of relative targets, state dates, deadline relations, comparison labels from the figures); a record with a script error goes to no AI check. The panel's own catch rate is measured by planted errors: before the pilot and after every ten instruments, a script copies one finished record, plants six errors of different kinds (a number, a date, a page, a direction, a status, an identifier digit) and keeps the key where no checker can read it; the copy goes through the scripts and the value check, and the share of planted errors caught is reported with each release. If the panel catches fewer than five of six, its prompts are revised on the development set only and the test is repeated. The panel decides nothing. The editor resolves every finding at the page and logs it; a finding that would change a status is decided by the editor at the page, flagged, and checked again by the AI auditor. The number of findings, by whether they change a status, a value or only wording, and the models used for each check are reported with each release. The start and end time of the editor's check are logged for each instrument.

**Establishing absence.** "No report found by the editor" needs four logged steps: the editor's search above; a request to the issuer for any public source, sent at least 15 working days earlier; an independent search by a second person (none in Release 2027.1, so such lines keep "No figure recorded yet"); and the editor's repeat search within 30 days before release. An AI search may add documents to be read but never counts as a search. A second person searches up to five lines per release; Release 2027.1 has no second person, so no line can complete the four steps.

**AI disclosure.** Each release states the provider, the model identifier as returned or shown, the route (programming interface, or chat product, with the memory, chat search and data-use settings and the terms version), the dates of the first and last runs, the settings sent or that they could not be set, the deposited prompt and fixed message, the pack search terms, the quote-check normalisation, and the number of runs made, repeated or discarded, with reasons. For every run, the prompt, the fixed message, the settings, the pack list (file hashes and page ranges) and the full output are deposited. Source files and document packs are not deposited; they can be retrieved and checked by URL and hash. A run in a chat product is a recorded procedure, not technical proof of what the AI was given.

### E.7 Technical validation

**AI blind coding.** An AI blind coder codes all instruments of at least three non-pilot groups drawn by published seed, covering at least eight promises, within the workload cap in Appendix D. It is a model other than the reading model (Appendix D), runs in a fresh context for each group, and is given only this Method with its appendices, the data dictionary, the entry sheet, the coder instructions (coder/CODER-INSTRUCTIONS.md) and the full files supplied, which are every document the editor read for those groups, with an index naming each document's type and no page references; it gets no web search, no record, no readings and no check-panel output. Its instructions are tuned on the reference cases and the pilot only, and frozen at protocol registration. Against the final record, and separately against the unchecked first reading, the release reports, with the raw tables: promise identification (promises found by both over promises found by either); exact matches for promised value, observation date, reported value, consequence, consequence taking effect, status and basis, each as "x of n" with an exact (Clopper-Pearson) 95% interval; and, for status, Krippendorff's nominal alpha (point estimate) and Gwet's AC1. Figures are labelled "agreement between an AI blind coder and the final record, indicative; not a human validation". Every difference is traced at the page by the editor and logged; a rule at fault is rewritten as a logged deviation. For exact match on status, "No figure recorded yet" and "No report found by the editor" count as one value.

**Validation of the AI reading.** The prompt is tuned on the development set (the reference cases whose websites allow AI use, the pilot, and a trap run in which a reference case is run without its results report) and frozen at protocol registration. The held-out set is the AI blind coder's groups. The answer key is built from the AI blind coder's sheet; where it differs from the final record, the editor decides at the page, seeing both values unlabelled, and the AI auditor checks that decision. Recall, accuracy and invented values of the unchecked first reading against this key are reported as "x of n" with exact 95% intervals, on the held-out set and the trap run, but because the key is made by an AI, the pre-registered marks are not applied and every release states "AI reading not validated". The same figures for the final record are reported alongside. The development set is re-run before further use after any change of the model identifier shown, the prompt, this Method or the route.

**Effect of the editor's check.** On the held-out set, against the AI answer key (so the figures are indicative only), the release reports, as "x of n" with the raw table, for core fields: (a) of values where the AI's first reading differs from the key, the number the editor corrected to the key value; (b) of values where the AI's first reading equals the key, the number the editor changed away from it; (c) of key promises missing from both AI readings, the number the editor added. (a) and (c) show how far the editor's check depends on the AI's reading. They are the measure referred to in limitation (7) and carry no pass mark.

**AI audit.** An AI auditor, a model other than the reading model and the AI blind coder, checks against the source, for every link, the core fields, unit, perimeter, reporting deadline, and status with basis; the other fields on ten random links; for links with basis "comparison of figures", the editor's statement-search log against the places listed in section 6; re-runs the frame steps from the registered files by script; and confirms the run log on three AI runs drawn at random. Where the two AI readings differed, it sees both values unlabelled as A and B, with the editor's choice. It works from the saved files in a fresh context and decides nothing: the editor resolves every finding at the page and logs it. If it reports more than two material errors (issuer, instrument, value, date, consequence or status) in lines the editor checked without change, the editor checks the release again in full. The editor approves the release; every count carries the label "Provisional. Read first by an AI system and checked by one editor. Not yet checked by a second person."

**Other measures.** Agreement between the two AI readings and between the AI's first reading and the final record, within the document pack (promise identification and exact match on core fields, with the raw table), the number of links flagged because a statement relies on a target changed outside the terms' clause, run-to-run stability (between the two readings of every instrument; the two readings use the same model and are not independent), the quote-check pass rate and the share of values changed by the editor are published with counts, without pass marks. If the final record proves wrong in more than 10 per cent of non-pilot core fields checked by the AI auditor, counted per field, every link is checked in full. Coding dates are recorded so that early and late error rates can be compared.

### E.9 Inference, reporting and limitations

**Inference.** The random sample supports statements about in-scope issuer groups in the frame and nothing wider. Shares by status are given per promise and per link, with both denominators, and with 95% intervals from a bootstrap resampling issuer groups (10,000 resamples), labelled approximate. Statistics on consequences count instruments. A statistic is published only once the random sample alone has at least ten groups; until then a release gives plain counts, labelled "random sample plus stratum; not representative of the frame".

**Reporting.** No per-issuer totals, and no colour, icon or order that ranks labels; others may compute totals under the licence and are asked to cite this Method and its limits. In a release without names, no breakdown by country, sector or other subgroup is shown, and a count covering fewer than five issuers is shown as "fewer than 5"; in a named release a subgroup is shown only with at least ten issuers. Every count in Release 2027.1 carries the label "Provisional. Read first by an AI system and checked by one editor. Not yet checked by a second person.", adding "Before issuer replies." while the reply window is open. The project's own text never uses the words listed in the wording rule of the project (Appendix C), except inside quotations and source titles. The editor is a founder of Greenhold, a separate company; the rules separating the two are published, and from sample publication until 30 days after each release the editor neither holds nor trades securities of issuers in the frame.

**Limitations.** (1) Coverage: the frame is the Euronext list on the download date; earlier redeemed or delisted bonds may be missing, and results do not describe European SLBs as a whole. (2) Timing: only targets observed in 2024 or 2025. (3) Self-report: the record repeats what issuers and their external reviewers published and cannot tell whether a published figure is correct; whether an issuer reports may depend on the result, so "No report found by the editor" is not evidence of either outcome. (4) Issuer input: replies can change records in one direction; such changes are flagged and counted. (5) Language: documents the editor does not read unaided are checked through the AI's translation; such lines are flagged, and errors of translation may differ by language. (6) Size: about ten groups in wave 1; all shares, agreement and validation figures are indicative, and promises within a group are not independent. (7) One editor, who wrote the rules and applies them, and who checks values the AI found first, so his check may lean toward the AI's reading; no outside person codes or audits Release 2027.1, so its blind coding and audit are made by AI models, the AI reading is not validated, and figures against the AI answer key are indicative only. (8) AI: outputs vary between runs and models are withdrawn, so a run may not be repeatable; runs are made in an account with memory and chat search as recorded, so earlier conversations may influence a run; the pack rule may leave out relevant pages.

Changes to this Method follow the public procedure in Appendix C; each release states the Method version used.

### E.10 Further coding rules

These rules settle cases found in testing; they apply with the rules of E.2 to E.7.

1. **Restructuring.** An instrument whose claims were converted into equity, replaced by new instruments or written off under a court-approved restructuring or insolvency plan (for example a French procédure de sauvegarde) counts as exchanged (rule 1) on the plan's effective date as stated by the issuer, even if part of the claim survives in another instrument. The state may be established by combining issuer documents, if one names the ISIN and another states the effect and date for the class of claims containing it; all are cited.
2. **Ended links.** A link that ends under rule 1 takes the consequence code "not applicable: instrument ended before observation date". Where every link of a promise ends under rule 1, the promise carries no further label and no observation is recorded; observations already found are kept as information only.
3. **Names.** The GLEIF legal name on the retrieval date and the name in the terms are both recorded; renamings are noted from GLEIF's history.
4. **Offering documents.** A preliminary or final offering circular or listing particulars published by the issuer or the listing venue counts as terms if its sustainability-linked terms match the instrument identified elsewhere (amount, maturity); a preliminary document is flagged "preliminary terms". A difference in amount caused only by an increase or reduction at pricing, stated by the issuer, does not prevent a match.
5. **Minimum search for terms.** Before "documents not obtained", the editor searches the issuer's investor or debt pages, the listing venue's page for the ISIN, the filings database of the regulator of the issuer's legal address and, if different, of the listing venue's State, and one web search on the ISIN, and logs each.
6. **Framework by reference.** A target the terms define, even by reference to a framework, is a promise; the terms' sentence and the framework sentence are both quoted.
7. **Relative targets.** A relative target is recorded as written (percentage change and direction of the change), with the baseline; an absolute equivalent may be added, marked "derived".
8. **Deadline.** Any date in the terms by which the issuer must publish, deliver or notify the target result counts as the reporting deadline.
9. **Confirmation documents.** Where the terms apply the payment change unless the issuer delivers a document they name (for example a notice to the paying agent), that document, or a notice of the new rate, is a statement; its absence is not. Such a document is a statement only if it is published; a private delivery is recorded as "stated as delivered, not seen".
10. **Which entities are sources.** Only documents of the issuer, a guarantor or the reporting entity named in the terms are sources; reports of other group entities are not.
11. **Reports known but not obtained.** A report known to exist but not obtained is logged "report not obtained"; the line takes "No figure recorded yet" until it is read and may never take "No report found by the editor".
12. **Announced recalculation.** Where the terms announce a recalculation and no issuer document states the recalculated value citing the clause, the original target is used and the link is flagged "announced recalculation: no recalculated value found by the editor".
13. **Search before rules 5 and 6.** Rules 5 and 6 both apply only after the logged search of section 6 found no statement.
14. **Archive.** The archive searched for the frame is the Internet Archive (web.archive.org), at the list's registered address; the capture used is the one defined in E.2 (the first dated on or after 31 December 2023).
15. **Statements about a class of instruments.** A statement that names no ISIN but describes a class of instruments (for example "bonds linked to sustainability targets whose step-up was triggered") counts as "stated as applied" for a link only if the class clearly contains that instrument and the statement is dated on or after that instrument's first payment date at the new rate; otherwise the link takes "announced" where an issuer document says the change will take effect, and the class statement is quoted in the notes.
